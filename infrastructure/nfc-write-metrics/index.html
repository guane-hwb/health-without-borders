    NFC Write Metrics

    How big the app's NFC writes are against the chips they go to: what the app reports, what the backend keeps, and how to read it before choosing a payload format.

    1. Why

    The app writes an encrypted payload to every chip it touches. The patient device gets a triage summary; the guardian device gets a copy of the record, bounded to the most recent consultations and vaccines and trimmed further until it fits the chip. Today every payload is format 1: CBOR, compressed with DEFLATE, encrypted with AES-256-GCM (wire format in NFC Key Management § 2). A format 2 is being designed.

    Choosing between them needs three numbers that only the field has: how big the writes are, how close they come to each chip's capacity, and how often the guardian copy has to leave history out to fit. A synthetic corpus can estimate them; this telemetry measures them.

    2. What the app reports

    POST /api/v1/patients/nfc-write-metrics, one entry per chip write, up to 5,000 entries per request:

    Field Type and unit Meaning
    device_role "patient" or "guardian"; default "patient" Which device was written. Any other value is stored as patient, so a future role never drops a batch.
    payload_format integer, 1–255 1 = CBOR + DEFLATE + AES-GCM (today's); 2 = the format under development.
    key_version integer, 0–255 NFC key version the payload was encrypted with.
    payload_bytes integer, 0–65,535 bytes The encrypted payload.
    message_bytes integer, 0–65,535 bytes The whole NDEF message written.
    chip_capacity integer, 0–65,535 bytes The NDEF capacity the chip reported.
    consultations_kept, consultations_total integer, 0–1,000, or null Guardian copy only: consultations written to the chip, out of how many the copy would carry on a chip with room for everything (the most recent, at most three). Null for a patient device.
    vaccines_kept, vaccines_total integer, 0–1,000, or null The same, for vaccines.
    written_at date-time with an offset The device's clock at write time. A value without an offset is refused (422): read as UTC, it would shift the write by the device's own offset.

    Allowed roles: doctor, nurse and org_admin (an org_admin device that signed in before that role stopped receiving keys keeps its ring until its session ends). The answer is 202 with received and stored; a value out of range, an empty batch or a missing field is a 422 for the whole batch.

    Not idempotent, by design. Every write is a sample and is appended. Nothing in an entry identifies the chip or the write (§ 3), so there is nothing to de-duplicate on, and two writes of the same size are two samples. A batch sent twice counts twice.

    Entries that contradict themselves are dropped, not stored: a message smaller than its payload, a message larger than the chip's capacity, a copy that kept more consultations or vaccines than its total, or a kept count without its total (or the reverse). No real write produces one; it can only come from a measuring bug, and storing it would bend the sizes everyone reads. The batch is still accepted (stored < received) so the device does not retry it, and the backend logs one warning per batch with the counts by reason, never the values:

    Dropped 3 NFC write metrics that contradict themselves (kept_over_total=1, message_over_capacity=2) reporter_id=… app_version=1.2.3+45 (android)

    A capacity of 0 is therefore consistent only with an empty message: any message larger than 0 bytes is larger than the chip.

    3. What is not collected

    • No chip UID, not even as an HMAC as the key-version sightings keep it. The question is about sizes, not chips: writes to one chip cannot be told apart from writes to several.
    • No patient identifier and no clinical content. The consultation and vaccine counts say how much history a guardian copy carried, not what it said.
    • The request schema has no field for any of these, and a field outside the schema is ignored (pydantic's default, as for the sightings): a device_uid or a patient_id sent by mistake never reaches the service, and the table nfc_write_metrics has no column for them (Database Schema § 2.7).

    The server adds four values from the request, not from the entry:

    Column Source
    reported_by The authenticated user.
    organization_id That user's organization; it limits an org_admin's summary (§ 5).
    app_version The X-App-Version header, read as login reads it: none when absent, invalid when it does not match the format.
    reported_at The server's clock when the batch arrived.

    The reporter and the time remain, as in the sightings: a row says which staff member wrote a chip of which kind, and when. Matched against the patient access log, a row could point at the record that had just been synced, which tells nothing the record itself does not hold. It is still one more reason the rows do not outlive the retention period.

    4. Retention

    Rows older than NFC_WRITE_METRIC_RETENTION_DAYS (365 days by default) are deleted whenever a batch is stored. Age is counted by reported_at, the server's clock: a write made long ago but reported today is kept a full period. A payload-format decision needs months of writes, not a history.

    The setting sits next to NFC_KEY_SIGHTING_RETENTION_DAYS in app/core/config.py and .env.example.

    5. Reading the summary

    GET /api/v1/patients/nfc-write-metrics/summary?window_days=90
    Role Sees
    superadmin Every organization.
    org_admin The writes reported by users of their own organization, as /stats/overview scopes it.
    doctor, nurse 403.

    window_days goes from 1 to 730 (default 90). Rows older than the retention period are already deleted, so a longer window reads the same as that period.

    The window is on written_at, the device's clock, as the key-version usage window is on observed_at: a brigade that was offline for a week reports its writes late, and they still count on the days they were made. A device with a skewed clock can move a write in or out of the window; it cannot change its size.

    Writes are grouped by device_role, payload_format and chip_capacity, and the groups are sorted in that order. Each group reports:

    Field Unit Meaning
    writes writes How many writes the group holds.
    message_bytes_median bytes Median NDEF message size (nearest rank, below).
    message_bytes_p90 bytes 90th percentile of the message size (nearest rank).
    message_bytes_max bytes Largest message.
    utilization_median_pct % of chip_capacity, one decimal Median of message_bytes / chip_capacity × 100; null when the chips reported a capacity of 0.
    trimmed_writes writes Writes where consultations_kept < consultations_total or vaccines_kept < vaccines_total.
    consultations_dropped consultations Sum of consultations_total − consultations_kept.
    vaccines_dropped vaccines Sum of vaccines_total − vaccines_kept.

    Nearest rank. The median and the p90 are the elements at index int(q × (n − 1)) of the sorted sizes, with q = 0.5 and 0.9: always a size some write really had. With an even number of writes the median is the lower of the two middle ones; with two writes, both are the smaller one. Read small groups with that in mind.

    What counts as trimmed. A guardian copy carries at most the three most recent consultations and the three most recent vaccines (health-without-borders-frontend, nfc_guardian_payload.dart), whatever the chip. The app reports as *_total what the copy would carry on a chip with room for everything — the record's count, capped at three — not the record's whole count. So kept < total means the chip's capacity left history out, and nothing else: a patient with five consultations whose copy kept its three most recent is not trimmed.

    6. How it informs the payload-format decision

    1. Does format 1 fit today's chips with room to spare? For each patient group, compare message_bytes_p90 and message_bytes_max with chip_capacity; utilization_median_pct is the typical fill. A p90 close to the capacity means the next field added to the triage payload will not fit on those chips.
    2. How much history do guardian copies lose? trimmed_writes / writes is the share of guardian writes that left something out, and consultations_dropped and vaccines_dropped say how much (§ 5 for what counts as trimmed).
    3. What does format 2 buy? Once builds that write it are in the field, compare its groups with the format 1 groups of the same device_role and chip_capacity: median and p90 sizes, fill and trimming. Same role, same capacity and a different format is the comparison that isolates the format.
    4. Which builds write which format? The summary does not split by build; the rows do. A reader that predates format 2 cannot open what it writes, so format 2 can only become the one written once every device that reads chips understands it: readers first, as with the keyring (NFC Key Management). On the database:
    SELECT app_version, device_role, payload_format, count(*) AS writes
FROM nfc_write_metrics
WHERE reported_at > now() - interval '30 days'
GROUP BY app_version, device_role, payload_format
ORDER BY app_version, device_role, payload_format;

    Limits. Only builds that report write metrics appear here, and app_version says which ones do. The sizes are measured by the app: the consistency checks drop impossible entries, but they cannot catch a measurement that is wrong in a plausible way.
